## 1. Title (max 30 characters)

Google Play's title field is capped at **30 characters** (reduced from 50 back in Sept 2021, and unchanged since — confirmed by multiple 2025/2026-dated ASO sources). It is the single most heavily weighted field for search relevance, so the primary keyword phrase should sit in it.

| # | Option | Chars |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ek Tap: 12 One-Tap Games** | 24 |
| 2 | Ek Tap - One Button Games | 25 |
| 3 | Ek Tap - 12 Mini Games | 22 |

**Recommended: Option 1 — `Ek Tap: 12 One-Tap Games`** (24/30 chars)

Reasoning:
- Leads with the brand name (`Ek Tap` — memorable, Urdu meaning doubles as a tagline).
- Packs in the two highest-value keyword phrases found in competitor research: **"one-tap"** (a whole naming sub-genre on Play — see `keywords.md`) and **"games"** (plural, matches how bundle apps title themselves).
- The **"12"** mirrors a real pattern seen in competing minigame bundles, which advertise their game count directly in title/description (e.g. "2-3-4 Player Mini Games", "Offline Games" ~1000+).
- No emoji, no "best/#1/free", no repeated punctuation — clean against Play's Metadata policy (see §8 below).

Option 2 targets the "one button" sub-niche directly (a real cluster: "Button.", "One More Button", "ONE BTN BOSSES") — a fallback if Option 1's exact phrase turns out contested at submission time. Option 3 drops the input-verb keyword in favour of the bundle-count framing alone; weakest of the three on keyword coverage.

## 2. Short description (max 80 characters)

Shown above the fold on the store listing page and indexed for search (2nd most-weighted field after title, per current ASO guidance).

> **`12 offline one-tap games. Hold, release, tap. No wifi needed. Play anywhere.`**

**76 / 80 characters.**

Covers: bundle count ("12"), primary keyword ("one-tap games"), the actual input verbs ("hold, release, tap" — a real, underused long-tail phrase per keyword research), and the strong recurring query pattern "no wifi" / "offline". No superlatives, no emoji, no exclamation-mark stuffing.

## 3. Full description (max 4000 characters)

**3,174 / 4000 characters.** Hook in the first 3 lines (visible before "Read more" truncation on most devices), one bullet per game, natural keyword repetition (not stuffed — "one tap"/"one-tap" appears 4×, "offline" 4×, "mini game(s)" 5× across ~3,200 characters, all in context, none in a bare repeated list). No "best/#1/top", no unverifiable superlatives, no store-performance claims, no misleading ad/IAP claims — checked against the Play Metadata policy and the Store Listing / Promotional Content policy.

```
Ek Tap means "one tap" in Urdu — because every game here is played with a single finger.

12 one-tap mini games in one small offline app. Hold and release, or just tap — that's the whole rulebook, twelve times over. No second input, no menus to learn, no wifi needed. Pick up your phone, play one round in 15 seconds, put it back down.

Every sprite is drawn in code, not downloaded art — colourful, minimal, and fast to load on any phone. Several games are built around everyday South Asian life: a roadside dhaba, a Basant kite fight, a busy bazaar, a hot tawa, a packed chowk crossing.

WHAT YOU'LL PLAY

- Almost Pop — blow up a balloon inside a ring of pins, let go before it touches
- Dhaba Pour — pour chai to the chalk line (tea still in the air lands after you release)
- Rope Race — climb a rope, rest before your grip runs out, beat your own ghost run
- Kite Cutter — pull your kite string tight, snap it the instant a rival kite crosses
- Tawa Flip — flip a roti off the charge dial so it lands flat in the shrinking zone
- Cricket Edge — time the swing: early for a big shot, on the edge for safe runs
- Orbit Sling — whip a stone around a planet and sling it on to the next one
- Scale Call — load a brass scale, let go before it looks level (it keeps swinging)
- Wire Walker — flip your balance pole, but only when the wind really needs it
- Kabaddi Raid — raid deep, tag defenders, get home before they catch you
- Bazaar Bell — haggle the price down, lock it in before the shopkeeper catches on
- Chowk Crossing — step across a busy intersection between rickshaws, bikes and a cow

WHY ONE TAP

Most reflex games and reaction-time games ask for two hands and a dozen buttons. Ek Tap strips every game down to one input, so the challenge is timing, not controls. Twelve different one-tap mini games means twelve different reasons to come back — a quick reflex round on the bus, a longer bazaar-haggling session on the sofa, a kite duel between meetings. Each mini game keeps its own best score on your device, so every replay is a race against yourself, not a leaderboard you'll never touch.

BUILT TO PLAY OFFLINE

Ek Tap needs no wifi, no login, and no account to start playing. Every mini game runs and saves its high score locally, so this offline arcade works on a flight, in a lift, in a village with no signal — anywhere a normal online game would just spin and fail to load. It's a genuinely no-wifi-needed casual game, not an offline mode bolted on to an online one.

WHO IT'S FOR

If you want a game you can explain in one sentence and hand to anyone — a kid, a grandparent, a friend waiting in line at the bank — Ek Tap is built for exactly that. No tutorials to sit through, no combos to memorise, no in-app currency to grind. It's a simple, easy-controls arcade game collection: hold, release, or tap, and you already know how to play all 12.

A FEW HONEST NOTES

Ek Tap is a free download supported by ads between rounds; there is no pay-to-win and no gameplay is locked behind a paywall. It's a small, independent project — 12 mini games, one shared save file, one app icon, no bloat.

Download Ek Tap and see how far one finger can take you.
```

16 characters (well under 30 — Urdu renders more compactly per character than English for this phrase).

## 5. Category + tags

- **Primary category: Games > Arcade.** Ek Tap's core loop (short reflex/timing rounds, replay-focused, no puzzle-solving or strategy layer) matches Arcade more precisely than Casual (broader, often includes idle/collection games) or Puzzle (implies logic/solving, which none of the 12 games require).
- Could not verify from live sources whether category choice has a direct ranking/algorithmic effect on Play's charts or search (flagged as unconfirmed in the ASO research pass) — treat category as a discovery/browse filter first, not a ranking lever.
- **Tags/content descriptors to set in Play Console:** Arcade, Casual, Offline, Single Player, Reflex.

## 6. Content rating notes — FLAG FOR KAMAL

- **Ads are present** (confirmed in the app description above and the README's stated monetization). This must be disclosed accurately in Play Console's Ads declaration.
- **Families / child-directed policy consideration:** the simple one-tap mechanic, bright code-drawn art, and "hand it to a kid" framing in the description make Ek Tap *look* like it could appeal to the Designed for Families program. **Do not opt into Ads for Families or the Families policy without a deliberate decision** — that program has its own stricter ad-network/SDK requirements (no behavioral ad targeting to children, certified families ad networks only) and COPPA-adjacent obligations. Recommend: **do not declare this app as primarily child-directed or opt into Families** unless Kamal has confirmed the ad SDK in use is Families-certified and he wants the compliance overhead. Default content rating should reflect a general-audience casual game, not a kids' app.
- No violence, gambling-mechanic, or user-generated-content concerns identified in the 12 games as described — should clear IARC content rating as low/general.

## 7. Screenshot plan (8 phone screenshots)

Play requires a minimum of 2 phone screenshots and allows up to 8; research confirms the first 2-3 are what drive most conversion, and that caption/text overlays showing real gameplay (not just menus) convert better than plain menu shots. Recommended order below front-loads variety and the South-Asian-flavour hook, per that guidance.

| # | Screenshot | Caption headline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kite Cutter** in action, kite string taut, rival kite crossing | "Snap the string at the perfect moment" |
| 2 | **Game picker / menu** grid showing all 12 games | "12 one-tap games. One small app." |
| 3 | **Dhaba Pour** mid-pour, chai arcing toward the chalk line | "Pour it perfect — even after you let go" |
| 4 | **Kabaddi Raid** mid-raid with defenders closing in | "Raid deep. Get home before they catch you." |
| 5 | **Tawa Flip** roti mid-air over the shrinking landing zone | "One tap. Perfect flip." |
| 6 | **Bazaar Bell** haggling meter with price ticking down | "Haggle it down. Lock it in." |
| 7 | **Rope Race** ghost-run overlay (your best vs. this run) | "Beat your own best run" |
| 8 | **Game-over / best-score card** on any game | "Every game, its own best score" |

General rules applied: gameplay over menus (screenshots 1, 3-7), the picker screenshot placed early to signal "12 games" bundle value (#2), a ghost/best-score screenshot to sell replayability (#7), and a closing screenshot on the shared save/scoring hook (#8). All captions are benefit-led, short, and avoid superlatives.

## 8. Feature graphic (1024×500 px)

**Concept:** A single wide banner split into a loose grid of small vignettes from 4-5 of the games (kite silhouette, tawa/roti, chai stream, kabaddi raid line, cricket bat edge) rendered in the same flat code-drawn art style, unified by one warm colour palette (terracotta/chai-brown, kite-red, sky-blue). Large centered wordmark **"Ek Tap"** with a small subtitle line **"12 one-tap games"** beneath it. No screenshot chrome (no phone bezel), no dense text, no claims — just the brand name, the game count, and a visual hint of the South-Asian flavour that differentiates it from generic Ketchapp-style bundles. This graphic doubles as the YouTube/video-trailer thumbnail fallback, so it needs to read clearly at small size, not just at full width.
